================================================================
Hymmnos Skill — With-Skill Translation (Eval 3)
================================================================

SENTENCE TO TRANSLATE:
  xE rre cloche cEzE hymmnos/.

================================================================
1. SENTENCE TYPE IDENTIFICATION
================================================================

This is a **Neopact Pastalie** (New Testament of Pastalie) sentence.

Diagnostic evidence:
  - It uses an **Emotion Verb** — `cEzE` — a verb whose template is `c.z.`
    (transform / become), with its two bank slots filled by the uppercase
    emotion vowels E and E. This is the hallmark of Pastalie grammar.
  - It opens with `xE`, the **subject definer** `x.` carrying one bank slot
    (here filled with vowel E), a construction that exists only in Pastalie.
  - It contains `rre`, the subject marker, used in non-first-person Pastalie
    sentences.
  - It terminates with `/.`, the Pastalie sentence-end marker that both closes
    the sentence AND invokes it (executes it as Song Magic).

Because an Emotion Verb is present, this is an **Emotion Verb sentence**
(感音句), used in METHOD_ songs on the Infel Phira server. Per the skill's
sentence-type guidance, I loaded references/grammar-pastalie.md.

================================================================

               |                       | (x. = singer's feeling about the
               |                       |  subject; E = joy/contentment)

               | (particle)            | grammatical subject (emphasized

================================================================
3. STRUCTURE
================================================================

This is a **non-first-person** Pastalie sentence. The template for such
sentences (from grammar-pastalie.md) is:

    [x.] + rre + [subject] + [Emotion Verb] + [object] + [compound]

Mapped to our sentence:

    xE          = [x.]              (singer's feeling about the subject)
    rre         = rre               (subject marker)
    cloche      = [subject]         (Cloche)
    cEzE        = [Emotion Verb]    (gladly becomes)
    hymmnos     = [object]          (song)
    /.          = terminator+invoke

Because the subject is a third person (Cloche, not "I"), the Emotion Verb's
Level-1 vowels undergo **direction shifting**: the Level-1 (self) vowels in
the verb now describe *the subject's* feelings rather than the singer's.
So the two E vowels in cEzE express that *Cloche* feels joy — Cloche is
glad/happy to become a song — while the E in xE separately expresses the
*singer's* feeling (the singer is happy about Cloche becoming a song).

================================================================
4. THE EMOTION VERB CONSTRUCTION IN DETAIL
================================================================

This is the core of Pastalie grammar and deserves close analysis.

4a. Template + Emotion Vowels
-----------------------------
Pastalie Emotion Verbs are built from two ingredients:

  1. A **template word** — a lowercase verb base dotted with "bank periods"
     (also called bank slots). Each period is a placeholder to be filled.
     Here the template is `c.z.` (pronounced スズ / suzu), meaning
     "transform, become". It has TWO bank slots.

     Template words documented in the lexicon include:
       h.m.m.r. (sing), c.z. (become), f.w.r.n. (embrace), d.z. (die),
       a.u.k. (to be), v.a (craft), r.f.m (see), s.l. (believe), etc.

  2. **Emotion Vowels** — uppercase vowels/letter-groups slotted into the
     bank periods. They encode WHO is being addressed and WHAT emotion is
     directed at them. There are three levels:

       Level 1 — toward SELF (the subject, after direction shift):
         A = power/eagerness/focus
         I = pain / want to flee / dread
         U = sadness / grief / worry
         E = joy / contentment / pleasure
         O = anger / aggression / cursing
         N = nothing / absent-minded / relaxed

       Level 2 — toward ANOTHER INDIVIDUAL:
         YA = want to serve / thinking of you
         YI = suffering / pain / death
         YU = sadness / anxiety
         YE = happiness / fortune
         YO = anger / rage
         YN = calmness / comfort

       Level 3 — toward the WORLD / SURROUNDINGS:
         LYA = thinking of all / want to devote myself
         LYI = pain / destruction / ruin
         LYU = sadness / instability
         LYE = happiness / satisfaction / prosperity
         LYO = strife / chaos / war
         LYN = calmness / quietness

4b. How cEzE is assembled
-------------------------
  Template:   c . z .          (c.z. = become, 2 bank slots)
  Slot 1  :   E                → joy (Level 1, toward the subject)
  Slot 2  :   E                → joy (Level 1, toward the subject)
  Result  :   c E z E          → written "cEzE"

  Reading the slots: bank slot 1 has the greatest effect/priority; lower
  indices carry stronger emphasis. Here both slots are E, so the emotion
  is uniform, unambiguous joy directed inward (at the acting subject).

  Per the skill's worked examples:
    c.z. + E, E  → cEzE   = "I gladly become"  (≈ "Was yea ra chs")
    c.z. + YE,YE → cYEzYE = "You become (and I want you to be happy)"
    c.z. + LYE,LYE → cLYEzLYE = targeting the world/situation

  Our cEzE is exactly the "E, E" case — "gladly becomes".

4c. Direction shifting (critical for this sentence)
---------------------------------------------------
In a FIRST-person sentence, Level-1 vowels describe the SINGER's own
emotion (cEzE alone = "I am delighted to become"). But our sentence is
non-first-person (subject = Cloche), introduced by the subject definer xE
and rre. Pastalie grammar therefore applies **direction shifting**: the
Level-1 vowels in the Emotion Verb are re-oriented to the SUBJECT.

  Result: the two E's in cEzE now mean that **Cloche** feels joy — Cloche
  is happy/glad to become a song. The singer's own emotion is NOT carried
  by the verb here; it is carried separately by the xE definer (next).

4d. Unused slots and minimalism
-------------------------------
Pastalie's design philosophy is maximum meaning in minimum words. Bank
slots that are not needed are simply omitted from the final word. c.z. has
only 2 slots and both are filled, so nothing is omitted. (Contrast
h.m.m.r. with 4 slots, where `hEmmErYE` fills slots 1,3,4 and leaves slot
2 empty, dropped silently.)

4e. Why the verb alone can be a sentence
----------------------------------------
When the object is implied by the emotion vowels, the Emotion Verb is a
complete sentence by itself (e.g., `fEwErYEn/.` = "I lovingly embrace
you"). Here an explicit object `hymmnos` (song) is supplied because the
target of the transformation is not implied by the vowels, so it must be
stated.

================================================================
5. THE SUBJECT DEFINER (x.) IN DETAIL
================================================================

The opening token `xE` is the **subject definer** `x.`, a Pastalie-only
particle that carries one bank slot for the singer's personal feeling
ABOUT the subject of the sentence. Its vowel meanings are distinct from
the Emotion Vowel levels:

  A = indifferent
  I = jealous
  U = concerned
  E = happy            ← our sentence
  O = angry
  N = opposed

So `xE` = "I (the singer) feel HAPPY about the subject (Cloche)."

This is separate from and in addition to the emotion encoded in the verb.
The sentence thus carries TWO emotional layers:
  (1) via xE — the singer is happy about Cloche;
  (2) via cEzE — Cloche herself is glad to become a song.

`rre` follows xE to mark `cloche` as the subject. The skill notes that
subject-form pronouns may omit `rre`, but using `rre` (especially with a
proper noun) emphasizes the subject.

================================================================
6. TRANSLATION
================================================================

Literal gloss:
  "I-feel-happy (about the subject) [marker] Cloche gladly-becomes song [invoke]"

English translation:
  "Cloche gladly becomes a song — and I am happy that she does."

Alternative renderings preserving nuance:
  - "Cloche is delighted to be transformed into song, and that makes me happy."
  - "Cloche joyfully turns into a song (and this brings me joy)."

Chinese translation (the skill notes Japanese meanings bridge to Chinese):
  「クローシェは喜んで詩（うた）になる——そして私はそれが嬉しい。」
  Cloche 喜悦地化作歌曲，而我也为此感到快乐。

Notes on what is and isn't translated:
  - The emotion encoded in the vowels (joy, in both the verb and x.) is
    rendered through word choice ("gladly", "delighted", "happy") rather
    than a separate prefix, since English/Chinese convey emotion through
    diction. This matches the skill's translation tip that emotion sounds
    / vowels are usually absorbed into wording rather than translated
    literally.
  - `/.` performs a real function (invoking the line as Song Magic) but
    has no English equivalent; it is reflected implicitly by the
    declarative force of the sentence.
  - Hymmnos has no tense; "becomes" / "will become" are both acceptable.
    Time is implied by context.

================================================================

================================================================
8. SUMMARY
================================================================

Sentence:  xE rre cloche cEzE hymmnos/.
Type:      Neopact Pastalie (Emotion Verb sentence, 感音句)
Structure: Non-first-person: x. + rre + subject + Emotion Verb + object + /.
Subject:   cloche (Cloche, proper noun)
Verb:      cEzE = c.z.(become) + E,E (joy, Level-1, direction-shifted to subject)
Object:    hymmnos (song)
Singer's feeling: xE = happy about Cloche (x. + E)
Terminator: /. = end + invoke as Song Magic

Translation:
  "Cloche gladly becomes a song — and I am happy that she does."

The emotion verb construction works as follows: the template c.z. ("become")
has two bank slots; each is filled with the Level-1 emotion vowel E (joy).
Because the sentence is non-first-person (subject = Cloche, marked by rre
and introduced by the subject definer xE), direction shifting reorients the
Level-1 vowels from the singer to the subject, so the two E's express that
CLOCHE herself is joyful about becoming. The singer's own emotion is
expressed independently and separately through xE (E = happy about the
subject). This dual-layer emotion encoding — singer's feeling in x., actor's
feeling in the verb — is the defining expressive power of Pastalie.

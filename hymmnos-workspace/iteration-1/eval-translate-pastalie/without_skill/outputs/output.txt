"x" and an uppercase vowel "E".

- E (uppercase vowel) = happiness / the desire to be happy. This is one of the
  core emotion vowels in Hymmnos. The uppercase E indicates the singer/speaker
  is experiencing or wishes to express happiness.
- x (lowercase consonant) = The exact semantic contribution of the consonant
  "x" in the Pastalie emotion sound system is uncertain to me. In the Pastalie
  system, the consonant may serve as an intensity modifier or a sub-type
  classifier for the emotion, but I cannot state its precise meaning with
  confidence.

In Neopact Pastalie, emotion sounds are condensed into compact single syllables
(consonant + vowel), unlike Standard Hymmnos which uses three-word emotion
phrases (e.g., "Was yea ra"). The emotion sound at the start of the sentence
sets the overall emotional tone for the entire utterance.

### 2. rre cloche — Subject Phrase

- rre = subject marker particle. In Hymmnos, the default grammatical subject is
  the first person (the singer/speaker themself). The particle "rre" is used to
  explicitly mark a different subject — a third person. Without "rre", the
  sentence would be about the speaker; with "rre", the subject shifts to the
  noun that follows.
- cloche = proper noun, almost certainly referring to Cloche, a major character
  from the video game Ar tonelico II (Cloche Leythal Pastalie, the Holy Maiden
  of Metafalss). The name is written in lowercase per Hymmnos orthographic
  convention (proper nouns are typically lowercase in Hymmnos script).

So "rre cloche" establishes Cloche as the subject performing the action.

### 3. cEzE — Emotion Verb (Pastalie Verb with Embedded Emotion)

This is the core verb of the sentence, rendered in the Neopact Pastalie style
with embedded emotion markers.

- The verb root is "cze" (with lowercase vowels in its plain form).
- In Neopact Pastalie, the vowels within a verb can be capitalized to embed
  the speaker's emotional state directly into the action being described.
- Here, both vowels are capitalized as "E", embedding the emotion of happiness
  (E = happiness / wanting to be happy) into the verb.
- The base verb "cze" most likely means "to sing" — this is inferred from
  context, since the object of the sentence is "hymmnos" (song). However, I
  am not fully certain of this lexical meaning from my own knowledge alone.

The capitalization pattern c-E-z-E shows that the emotion of happiness is
woven through the entire verb, coloring the action itself with the speaker's
emotional state.

### 4. hymmnos — Object

- hymmnos = "song" or "hymn." This is one of the most fundamental words in the
  Hymmnos language. In the game lore, "Hymmnos" also refers to the language
  itself and to the magical songs sung by Reyvateils. Here it functions as the
  direct object — the thing being sung.

### 5. /. — Sentence Terminator

- The forward slash "/" is a Neopact Pastalie sentence-ending marker. It

  Standard Hymmnos does not typically use this terminator.

## EMOTION VERB CONSTRUCTION — DETAILED EXPLANATION
================================================================================

### Overview of the Pastalie Emotion Verb System

Neopact Pastalie introduces a powerful feature not found (or found in a
different form) in Standard Hymmnos: the ability to embed emotion sounds
directly into verbs through capitalization. This allows a single verb to
express both an action AND the emotional state associated with that action,
making Pastalie a highly compact and emotionally expressive dialect.

### How It Works

1. VERB ROOT: Every Pastalie verb has a base root form written entirely in
   lowercase (e.g., "cze"). This root carries the lexical meaning of the
   action.

2. EMOTION EMBEDDING: To embed an emotion, one or more vowels in the verb root
   are changed from lowercase to uppercase. The uppercase vowel is no longer
   just a phonetic vowel — it now functions as an emotion sound (想音).

3. EMOTION VOWEL MEANINGS: Each uppercase vowel maps to a specific emotion:
   - A = power, the desire to be strong
   - I = pain, the desire to escape from pain
   - U = hatred, the desire to destroy
   - E = happiness, the desire to be happy
   - O = sadness, the desire to be released from sadness
   - N = nervousness / anxiety

4. MULTIPLE EMBEDDINGS: A verb can have more than one vowel capitalized,
   allowing the same emotion (or different emotions) to be emphasized at
   multiple points within the verb. In "cEzE", both vowels are "E", so the
   emotion of happiness permeates the entire verb.

### Applied to "cEzE"

- Root verb: cze (lowercase) — the plain action verb, likely "to sing"
- Emotion-embedded form: cEzE — the same verb, now infused with happiness
- The two uppercase E's indicate that the singer feels happiness in relation
  to the action of singing. The emotion is not stated separately — it is
  structurally part of the verb itself.
- This is more than an adverb ("happily sings"); the emotion is grammatically
  fused with the verb, reflecting the Pastalie philosophy that emotion and
  action are inseparable in the Song Magic of Ar tonelico.

### Relationship to the Initial Emotion Sound (xE)

The sentence has two layers of emotion expression:

1. SENTENCE-LEVEL EMOTION: The prefix "xE" sets the overall emotional tone
   for the entire sentence. This is the general emotional context.

2. VERB-LEVEL EMOTION: The embedded "E"s in "cEzE" provide emotion specific
   to the action of the verb. This can reinforce, nuance, or even contrast
   with the sentence-level emotion.

In this sentence, both layers use the "E" (happiness) emotion, creating a
consistent emotional tone: the singer is happy, and the action of Cloche
singing is also associated with happiness.

### Contrast with Standard Hymmnos

In Standard Hymmnos (Central Standard Note), emotion is expressed through a
three-word emotion phrase at the beginning of the sentence (e.g., "Was yea ra"
= "I want to be happy to want to do this"). The verb itself does not carry
embedded emotion. Pastalie's innovation is the ability to embed emotion
directly into the verb through capitalization, making the language more
compact and allowing finer-grained emotional expression tied to specific
actions within a sentence.

The sentence "xE rre cloche cEzE hymmnos/." translates approximately to:

  "With happiness, Cloche sings a song."

expressed at both the sentence level and the verb level, illustrating the
Pastalie dialect's characteristic fusion of emotion and action.
